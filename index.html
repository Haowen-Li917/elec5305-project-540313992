Project proposal · Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing

When should a denoiser adapt?

This project measures how quickly an STFT speech enhancer should update its estimate of background noise—and how that speed changes the balance between noise tracking and preserving speech.

Research focus: noise PSD smoothing factor α
01 · Research question

Track noise without erasing speech

How does the adaptation rate of an STFT noise power spectral density (PSD) estimator affect its ability to follow changing background noise, and what trade-off does faster tracking create between noise reduction and unintended suppression of speech?

P̂n(k,m) = α P̂n(k,m−1) + (1−α)|Y(k,m)|²

Lower α reacts faster to new observations. Higher α smooths more but can lag when noise changes.

Noisy speech→STFT→Noise PSD estimate→Wiener gain→Enhanced speech

When the voice activity detector identifies a noise-only frame, the recursive estimate updates. During speech, the estimator is held. A VAD ablation will reveal what happens when speech contaminates the noise estimate.

02 · Experimental design

A controlled parameter study

Baseline

Fixed α = 0.98

Reference setting for exponential noise PSD smoothing and a Wiener filter. Other processing settings stay fixed in the alpha sweep.

Conditions

Stationary and changing noise

Compare constant noise with step changes. Test changes during pauses and active speech, with VAD-gated and ungated updates.

Sweep

α = 0.80 to 0.995

Compare 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 0.98, and 0.995. Plot tracking error against speech retention.

Tracking

PSD error and recovery

Measure relative RMSE against known noise PSD and frames to recover within 10% of the new level.

Preservation

Speech retained

Measure clean-speech energy retained, STOI, and segmental SNR on paired signals.

Dataset

VoiceBank+DEMAND

Planned paired speech evaluation alongside synthetic step noise where ground truth is known.

03 · Early evidence

A small synthetic MATLAB pilot

The MATLAB R2024a script was run on a deterministic single-bin simulation with 1,200 frames, three noise power levels, two speech intervals, oracle activity labels, and seed 540313992. The table shows relative PSD RMSE on noise-only frames, clean-speech energy retained, and noise energy remaining during speech.

αNoise PSD relative RMSE ↓Speech energy retained ↑Noise energy remaining ↓
0.8036.180%30.141%31.259%
0.9537.309%24.590%26.031%
0.98 · baseline51.074%22.271%23.939%
0.99564.297%23.208%25.489%
What this suggests: In this pilot, lower α tracked the imposed changes more closely; α = 0.98 left less noise during speech frames but retained less clean-speech energy.

This is a preliminary synthetic result, not a listening test, a full STFT experiment, or a result from recorded speech. It uses one frequency bin, one seed, and oracle labels. No general performance claim follows from this pilot.

04 · Reproducibility

Run the pilot and extend it

  1. Use MATLAB R2024a or later; the pilot requires no external audio or toolbox.
  2. From the repository root, run run('experiment/pilot_alpha_sweep.m').
  3. Record the MATLAB release and verify the printed table against the prototype values.
  4. For the full study, document files, sample rate, STFT window and hop, VAD settings, noise-change times, and seeds. Keep speech and noise separate for reference metrics.
